Before you go

The travel pass

Sixteen moving days favour a national pass over single tickets. Consecutive passes cap at 15 days — buy that plus one cheap last-day ticket, or take the Flex pass (chosen days within a month), which wins if you sit still for the Zurich cluster around the Parade. Price both before buying.

Renting gear

Transa rents tents, bags, and mats from its city branches — pick up the afternoon before, return the afternoon after. For Lac Bleu, take a cold-rated bag (around −5°C), an insulated pad you won't regret, a light tent over a bivy, and a gas stove. Carry everything out.

The bivouac rule

At Lac Bleu: one night, above the treeline, up at dusk and down at dawn, no trace, no fire. It's a tolerated grey zone — keep it solo and quiet. If the weather turns, Camping Arolla sits just below, fully legal, for a few francs.

Cold water

Lac Bleu is glacial — single digits even in August. Wade, stay shallow, do it in daylight while warm from the hike, and dry off fast. The Verzasca pools run cold with strong current. Lungern, Obersee, and Oeschinensee are the friendly swims.

What's out there

No resident bears. Wolves live in Valais but avoid people — not a tent concern. Keep food sealed and away from where you sleep, and you'll have a quiet night.

Book early

Arolla lodging and gear rental fill up across peak summer. Reserve both well ahead — and the Oeschinensee lakeside pitch too, if you take the packed route.
